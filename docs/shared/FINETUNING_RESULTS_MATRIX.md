# In-domain fine-tuning — the results matrix

> **Status:** current — paper-facing reference, 2026-11-23. This document owns the cross-task
> presentation only; every number below belongs to the result note named beside it, and that note
> remains the authority. The tables are generated by
> `scripts/analyses/finetuning_results_matrix.py`; a test fails when they drift.

**Question.** Across lapse, frequency and severity, what did the in-domain fine-tuning programme
run — on which target, over how many rows, with which splits, seeds, arms and epochs — and what did
each registered target decide?

**Answer.** The programme is closed out at its registered designs: every target below has a verdict,
and one further classification target was stopped on resolution and is not in this matrix. The tasks
conclude differently. **Lapse:** one small gain and two equivalences, each on a stratified holdout
per seed. **Frequency:** a task-level Go carried by the French portfolio; Spanish is inconclusive
and underpowered by design. **Severity:** no task-level claim — French harm, Spanish inconclusive at
the registered rung, and the 30-epoch Spanish gain exploratory.

## How to read it

- **One model line.** Every fine-tuning cell is `v3_default` (tabpfn 8.5.0, with the checkpoint each
  capture records). There is no v3.5 fine-tuning evidence in this repository, and a v3 number is not
  evidence about v3.5 ([`MODEL_VERSIONS.md`](../MODEL_VERSIONS.md)).
- **In-domain only.** Each run trains on the target dataset's own training rows. The pooled and
  transfer arms were never executed; they are untested, not failed
  ([Phase 2 §4](../experiments/pilot2-phase2/FINETUNING_PHASE2_OUTCOME.md#4-four-ways-to-misread-this)).
- **Both arms are TabPFN.** Every estimate is tuned against raw on the same seeds and launch. It is
  the value of fine-tuning, not of TabPFN against a GLM or CatBoost. The actuarial baseline answers
  that other question: when the baselines may use all the data — the registered primary comparison —
  CatBoost wins on every target; at the matched 10,000-row budget French frequency is too close to
  call ([`actuarial_baseline_summary.md`](../experiments/fair-test-freq-sev/actuarial_baseline_summary.md)).
- **Eligible is not training.** The eligible column is the prepared dataset; the training column is
  the registered cap. French frequency has 678,013 eligible policies and trains on 10,000; Spanish
  severity has 5,950 eligible claiming policies and trains on 4,760, the data's cap. The ledger's
  `share_of_eligible` records the fraction per row.
- **Lapse is not folded.** Each seed is its own stratified train/test split and its own fit, not a
  cross-validation fold; the between-seed interval therefore carries split variance as well as fit
  variance ([`data/README.md`](../analyses/data/README.md)). `uslapseagent` and `eudirectlapse` use
  10,000 train / 1,000 test rows per seed; `spanish_motor_lapse` uses 10,000 / 10,000. Writing
  "5-fold" there would be wrong.
- **The lapse primary estimand is unreported.** The statistical analysis plan names the paired
  difference in log loss as primary. Phase 2 reports ROC AUC with a Brier veto; no log-loss figure
  was computed, and that gap is stated rather than treated as a null
  ([Phase 2 §4](../experiments/pilot2-phase2/FINETUNING_PHASE2_OUTCOME.md#4-four-ways-to-misread-this)).
- **`coil2000` is not a lapse target.** It predicts caravan-insurance take-up and was stopped on
  resolution (best half-width ±0.0149 against a 0.006 floor), not measured and found null.
- **Severity has two units.** French severity is per-claim RMSE; Spanish severity is claim-weighted
  per-policy RMSE. There is no pooled severity number to quote
  ([`EPOCH_LADDER_RESULT.md`](../experiments/pilot2-phase2/EPOCH_LADDER_RESULT.md), #729).
- **The ladder is exploratory.** The statistical analysis plan lists the epoch ladder as
  exploratory, with no decision role. Table 2 adds the dose axis; the Spanish 30-epoch gain is not a
  powered decision test, and its shape is non-monotone (10 epochs is worse than 3). Table 2 names
  the ladder's target ids (`severity_french`, `severity_spanish`); they are `freMTPL2sev` and
  `spanish_motor_severity`. The ladder and the #572 row-scaling captures sit outside the runs
  ledger; its Scope names both.
- **Spanish frequency is underpowered by design.** Its MDE (4.69%) is above its own 4% floor, so a
  true 4% effect would be missed roughly half the time. The frequency Go rides on the French target
  under the registered pooled rule.

## How to check us

- `python scripts/analyses/finetuning_results_matrix.py --check` — the document and CSV match the
  generator.
- `pytest tests/test_finetuning_results_matrix.py` — each quoted number is checked against the
  owner's own row, the design axes are checked against the ledger, and the ladder's absence from the
  ledger is deliberate.
- Follow a row's `source` to the owner, and the owner's evidence section to the committed capture.

## Reproduce

```
python scripts/analyses/finetuning_results_matrix.py --write
```

It computes the fair-test and row-scaling cells from the committed captures through
`freq_sev_fair_test.build_summary()`, and quotes the lapse and ladder cells from their result notes;
a test checks each quoted cell against the owner. No run, no spend.
