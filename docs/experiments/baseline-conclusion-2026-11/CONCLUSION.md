# TabPFN on insurance data — the November 2026 baseline conclusion

> **Status:** current — 2026-11-21. The phase's closing statement, owned by this document.
>
> **Every number here belongs to the model version that produced it.** The frontier verdicts are
> the v3 line (`v3_default`); table 2 carries its version in every row; the Phase 2 summary it cites
> ran on `v3_default`. No v3 number is evidence about v3.5.

## 1. The answer

**TabPFN is the strongest risk-ranker on these datasets and is not the best pricer at scale.**

On the classification and lapse targets it is the best method on three and inside fold noise on two,
so it holds the frontier on five of the six; it is off the frontier on `ausprivauto0405`, where a GLM
has the lower log loss while TabPFN keeps the better AUC. It loses the count and severity targets to
trees.

Against a GLM that treats exposure the actuarial way, it loses log loss on `norauto` — both versions,
by a small margin on v3.5 — and on `ausprivauto0405` at v3; its AUC lead survives everywhere except
`norauto`.

On French frequency at 1,000–5,000 training rows, nothing beat the intercept: not a GLM, not CatBoost.
No TabPFN arm was run at those budgets — that comparison is stage 2, and it is not funded.

The short version for an actuary: use it for triage and ranking, keep a GLM or a GBDT for pricing at
scale, and expect the count verdict to be re-tested when the next model version lands.

## 2. Should you care?

If your question is **"who is riskiest?"** — lapse, claim propensity, surrender, triage — this
evidence says the model is the best ranker available to us on most of these datasets, and it needed
no tuning to get there.

If your question is **"how much will we pay?"** — frequency, severity, cost per policy — it says the
opposite: trees win, and a correctly specified linear model wins more often than the 2026-08 report
said.

## 5. Four ways to misread this

1. **Rates are not counts.** The external replication's exposure-weighted GLM reads 0.296 on
   `freMTPL2` — its own Table 1, on v2.6 and v3, scoring claim *rates*. Our frontier scores counts and
   records 0.32109 for its Poisson GLM. The levels are not comparable; only the ordering is.
2. **Inconclusive is not "no difference".** Five row-curve cells have a margin below the MDE of the
   best arm, and on the count targets at 1,000 rows the MDE itself is 2.7–13.1% of the mean. Those
   cells say the design could not separate the methods; they do not say the methods are equal.
3. **A null model winning is not a bug.** On French frequency the intercept beats every fitted model
   at 1k–5k rows. That is a statement about the data: at that budget there is no extractable signal,
   and a ten-parameter fit on ~50 claim events does harm.
4. **Fine-tuning not helping here does not mean it never helps.** Phase 2 (`v3_default`) found a small
   gain on one anchor, equivalences on two, and stopped one dataset on resolution — not a uniform
   null.

## 6. The direction this phase hands to

The phase closed the drift story first, then measured the baseline curve, because the drift claim was
already in the conclusion and a complete claim is one run to re-test rather than an argument. The
count question was not taken: it is deferred to the next model release, for the reason in the gap
register. What was not chosen is therefore visible rather than omitted.

## 7. What would change our mind

- **A count win.** A two-stage model beating single-stage on zero-inflated counts, on either the
  synthetic protocol or `bemtl97_amount`, would turn the pricing verdict.
- **A ranking loss against an exposure-aware GLM on a second dataset.** `norauto` is currently the
  only one where AUC goes; a second would make "best ranker" conditional on the book.
- **A measurable model-line alternative.** One fold of a second foundation-model family at a
  resolvable MDE would make "one family only" a choice rather than a constraint.
- **A new model version.** §15.1 writes the trigger as "a new `model_path` beyond `v3_default`"; any
  new family re-opens every verdict here.
